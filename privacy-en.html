Privacy statement

Last updated: 4 October 2026 · English translation added 11 October 2026

Vinventory is an app for keeping track of your wine cellar. This page explains which data the app uses, why, who holds it and what you can do with it yourself.

This is a translation of the Dutch version. If the two differ, the Dutch text applies.

Who is responsible?

Vinventory is offered as a hobby project by the administrator of github.com/Vinventory/vinventory.github.io. See Contact at the bottom.

Which data does the app use?

  • Your account: your email address and your password. The password is not stored in readable form.
  • Your cellar: everything you enter yourself: wines, quantities, prices, ratings, descriptions, photos of labels, reminders and logbook notes, including the names of people you drank a bottle with. You decide what you enter and you are responsible for it.
  • On your device: the app keeps your login session and your preferences (appearance, language, sorting, hidden reminders) in your browser and, if you enter it, your Claude key. There are no tracking cookies.

The app uses no analytics or advertising services and does not sell any data.

What for?

Only to make the app work: signing in, saving your cellar and showing it on all your devices. That is the service you ask for when you create an account. Scanning with Claude is optional: it only works if you enter your own key.

Who holds your data?

  • Supabase: your account and your cellar are in a Supabase database, in a data centre in West Europe (Ireland). See supabase.com/privacy.
  • GitHub Pages: the website is served by GitHub. When you open the site, GitHub may process technical data such as your IP address.
  • Google Fonts and jsDelivr: the app loads a font from Google Fonts and a code library from jsDelivr. Those services receive technical data such as your IP address.
  • Anthropic (Claude), only if you scan or have a description written: your browser sends the photo or text directly to Anthropic with your own key. The administrator of Vinventory does not see this, and your key never ends up in the database. The terms and privacy policy of Anthropic and of your account there apply to that data. Do not send photos with people in them.

Some of these services are (partly) located in the United States.

How safe is it?

  • The connection is encrypted (HTTPS).
  • The database is set up so that every user can only read and change their own wines. Other users cannot see your cellar.
  • The administrator does have access to the database's admin panel and can therefore technically view data. This only happens for maintenance or in case of a fault.

How long do we keep your data?

For as long as you have an account. If you delete your account, your account and all your wines are removed from the database immediately. Your Claude key disappears from your device as soon as you sign out. A backup file that you downloaded yourself belongs to you and is no longer in the app.

What can you do yourself?

  • View and take along: download a backup via Settings, then Backup. That file contains all your wines.
  • Change: edit your wines in the app.
  • Delete: Settings, then Account and Delete account.
  • Objection or complaint: get in touch (see below). You can also lodge a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

Contact

Questions? Open an issue at github.com/Vinventory/vinventory.github.io/issues. Note: issues on GitHub are public. Do not put personal data in them. What you want to do with your data (view, change, delete) you can largely do yourself in the app.

Changes

If something important changes in how the app handles data, this text will be updated and the date at the top will change.
