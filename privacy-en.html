Privacy statement

Last updated: 14 October 2026 · English translation added 11 October 2026

Vinventory is an app for keeping track of your wine cellar. This page explains which data the app uses, why, who holds it and what you can do with it yourself.

This is a translation of the Dutch version. If the two differ, the Dutch text applies.

Who is responsible?

Vinventory is offered as a hobby project by the administrator of github.com/Vinventory/vinventory.github.io. See Contact at the bottom.

Which data does the app use?

  • Your account: your email address and your password. The password is not stored in readable form.
  • Your cellar: everything you enter yourself: wines, quantities, prices, ratings, descriptions, photos of labels, reminders and logbook notes, including the names of people you drank a bottle with. You decide what you enter and you are responsible for it.
  • Friends and tips (only if you use them): a display name that you choose yourself, who is friends with whom, blocked users, invitations and the tips that friends send each other. A tip contains a few details of a wine (name, producer, vintage, region, country, grape, type, description and dishes) and, if the sender chooses, the label photo and their rating, plus a short message. Price, logbook, memories and "received from" are never included. The name of the shop where you bought the wine is included unless the sender switches that off (it is on by default); a map link is never included. Your friends see your display name, but never your email address or your cellar.
  • Messages with an animation (only if you use them): if you send a friend a message with an animation, we keep who sent it, to whom, which animation it is, a short message (if you add one) and when it was viewed. A viewed message is cleared away after 30 days.
  • Reports: if you report misuse, we keep your report, who you report and the reason, so that misuse can be prevented.
  • Free scans: a counter per account (how many free scans you used and when), only to keep track of the allowance and of misuse.
  • On your device: the app keeps your login session and your preferences (appearance, language, sorting, hidden reminders) in your browser and, if you enter it, your Claude key. There are no tracking cookies.

The app uses no analytics or advertising services and does not sell any data.

What for?

Only to make the app work: signing in, saving your cellar and showing it on all your devices, and, if you want, friends and tips. That is the service you ask for when you create an account. Scanning with Claude is optional: it only works if you enter your own key. You only make friends with an invitation that you share yourself; there is no search by name or email address.

Who holds your data?

  • Supabase: your account and your cellar are in a Supabase database, in a data centre in West Europe (Ireland). See supabase.com/privacy.
  • GitHub Pages: the website is served by GitHub. When you open the site, GitHub may process technical data such as your IP address.
  • Google (Gmail), for email: the app's emails (confirming your account and resetting your password) are sent through a Gmail account of the app. Your email address and the text of that email pass through Google. See policies.google.com/privacy.
  • Google Fonts and jsDelivr: the app loads a font from Google Fonts and a code library from jsDelivr. Those services receive technical data such as your IP address.
  • Anthropic (Claude), only if you scan or have a description written: if you entered your own key, your browser sends the photo or text directly to Anthropic with your own key. The administrator of Vinventory does not see this, and your key never ends up in the database. If you use the free scans, the photo or text goes through a function of the app at Supabase to Anthropic, with the key of the app. The photo is not stored there; we only remember how many free scans you used and when, to prevent misuse. The terms and privacy policy of Anthropic and of your account there apply to that data. Do not send photos with people in them.

Some of these services are (partly) located in the United States.

How safe is it?

  • The connection is encrypted (HTTPS).
  • The database is set up so that every user can only read and change their own wines. Other users cannot see your cellar; friends only see what you send to them yourself.
  • The administrator does have access to the database's admin panel and can therefore technically view data. This only happens for maintenance or in case of a fault.

How long do we keep your data?

For as long as you have an account. If you delete your account, your account, all your wines, your friendships, your invitations, your blocks, your messages with animation, reports by you or about you and the tips you sent or received that were still open are removed from the database immediately. A tip that someone already saved to their wishlist is their own copy and stays with them. A friendship or an open tip also disappears if you or your friend end the friendship or block. Your Claude key disappears from your device as soon as you sign out. A backup file that you downloaded yourself belongs to you and is no longer in the app.

What can you do yourself?

  • View and take along: download a backup via Settings, then Backup. That file contains all your wines.
  • Change: edit your wines in the app, and your display name for friends under Settings, Friends.
  • Friends and misuse: under Settings, Friends you remove or block a friend. For a tip, choose Report or block.
  • Delete: Settings, then Account and Delete account.
  • Objection or complaint: get in touch (see below). You can also lodge a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

Contact

Questions? Open an issue at github.com/Vinventory/vinventory.github.io/issues. Note: issues on GitHub are public. Do not put personal data in them. What you want to do with your data (view, change, delete) you can largely do yourself in the app. Misuse by another user you report in the app (Report or block on a tip); only the administrator reads that report.

Changes

If something important changes in how the app handles data, this text will be updated and the date at the top will change.
